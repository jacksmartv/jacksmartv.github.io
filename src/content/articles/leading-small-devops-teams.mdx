Most writing about platform migrations assumes a team large enough to run the migration as a dedicated workstream. In practice, a lot of meaningful infrastructure work — including the cloud-native migration described elsewhere on this site — happens with two or three engineers who are also keeping the existing platform running.

## Automation isn't optional when the team is small

With three DevOps engineers responsible for a 200+ microservices environment, manual processes don't scale linearly with effort — they scale with headcount, which wasn't growing. Every recurring manual task was a candidate for automation, not because automation is a best practice in the abstract, but because it was the only way the team's capacity matched the platform's size.

## Standards do the work that a bigger team would do with reviews

A small team can't review every change the way a larger platform team might. What worked instead was investing early in clear standards — CI/CD as the only path to production, Terraform as the source of truth for infrastructure — so that consistency came from the process, not from someone catching deviations after the fact.

## Mentoring at this scale looks different

Leading two or three engineers isn't about org design — it's close, hands-on collaboration on real production problems. The most effective mentoring happened during incident response and architecture reviews, not in separate training sessions.

## The honest trade-off

A small team moves slower on any individual initiative than a larger one would. What it gains is shared context — everyone on a three-person team understands the whole platform, which turned out to matter more than raw throughput during a migration where judgment calls came up constantly.
